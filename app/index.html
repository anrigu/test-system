      How it works. A face-landmark model finds 478 points on each parent. Their face shapes are mixed feature by feature, then reshaped with measured growth patterns: babies get a larger forehead and eyes, a shorter nose and a rounder jaw; puberty lengthens the jaw and nose for boys and fills the lips for girls; later decades add sagging, thinner lips, lines and grey hair. Skin and colour come from both photos, blended on your phone's graphics chip.

      This is a forecast for fun, not a genetic test. Real children mix their parents in ways no photo can predict.
